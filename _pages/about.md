Hi! I'm Aditya, a 4th-year Computer Engineering student at the University of Waterloo.

At the core of my research interests is a simple question: **How can we build models which achieve generalizable reasoning without sacrificing representative efficiency?** LLMs, while impressive on many simple tasks, often trade off efficiency for scale, relying on brute-force memorization rather than structured abstraction.

I'm particularly interested in the intersection of **reinforcement learning and representation learning** as a pathway toward scalable, reasoning-capable systems. Through research in **spatial AI and world modeling for robotics**, as well as **high-fidelity synthetic data generation for medical imaging**, I've developed a deep appreciation for the representation learning techniques that underpin modern generative models, such as diffusion and masked modeling.

Further, I believe that realizing the full potential of these models requires a joint focus on **representational efficiency and hardware-software codesign**. Motivated by recent work on ternary quantized networks, which remain underutilized on GPU hardware, I’m currently developing a deployment framework for such models on FPGAs, designing custom kernels for matrix operations and nonlinearities. This Fall, I’ll be joining Cerebras Systems as a Research Engineer Intern, where I’ll work on **knowledge distillation and Mixture-of-Experts** techniques to learn sparse, modular representations that can scale reasoning while maintaining efficiency.

Looking forward, I’m excited to push the boundaries of reasoning, generalization, and efficiency, especially in reinforcement learning settings grounded in physical systems like embodied AI and autonomous decision-making. I plan to pursue graduate studies starting in 2026 and am actively exploring labs working at this intersection. If you know of one, I’d love to connect!
